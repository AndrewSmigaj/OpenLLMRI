### Proposals (Claude)

Everything below the rules: the species, where each lives, what it yields, and the honest push-back on
lizards. **Interior Alaska has no reptiles or amphibians active in winter** (wood frogs are frozen solid
under the leaf litter); the small things you can catch in December are grubs in rotten wood, voles under
the snow, and red squirrels at their middens. Everything Andrew asked for is here except the lizards,
which are replaced by what is really there.

## 3. In one paragraph

The valley is not empty in December; it is quiet. Berries that ripened in August are still on the bush
under the snow, sour and frozen and edible, with one red kind that will make you sick. Spruce needles
make a tea; the inner bark of spruce and birch is famine food; chaga grows on the old birch. Hares run
the willow thickets and leave tracks a snare can be set across; ptarmigan and spruce grouse sit in the
trees and on the snow, tame enough that a thrown rock will miss three times and then not; red squirrels
have cached spruce cones in middens you can raid; voles tunnel under the snow; fish hang under the lake
ice. Wolves cross the valley and are heard before they are seen; a moose owns the trail; ravens find
your cache before you do. Digging for roots means digging frozen ground, which is its own honest
obstacle — and the one root at the marsh edge that looks like a carrot can kill.

## 4. The design

### 4.1 The rules
- **Real, in December.** Every living thing here exists in interior Alaska in early winter, in the form
  it takes then: frozen berries, dormant plants, the animals that stay. Nothing is added because it
  would be convenient.
- **Wildlife is events and sign** (Andrew): tracks, calls, a flock that bursts, a hare in the snare, a
  moose on the trail. Animals are not standing objects in a room except when present as a class (a flock,
  a squirrel at its midden) that yields an individual when acted on.
- **Foraging is work with feedback**: picking, digging, throwing, setting, checking — attended activities
  with honest misses and partial yields, never free.
- **Food yields are authored per item** (document 10 owns the numbers; document 23 owns what exists).

### 4.2 Flora — what a survivor can find (proposal)
| thing | where (documents 01/rooms.md) | December state | use | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| lowbush cranberry (lingonberry) | tussocks, spruce floor, the birch stand edge | red, frozen on the plant under snow | food — sour, small handfuls; a real calorie trickle | keeps all winter |
| crowberry | muskeg, ridge | black, frozen, bland | food — bulk, low value | easy to find, easy to overeat |
| highbush cranberry | willow bars, creek | red, on the bush above the snow | food — very sour; a few handfuls; smells odd | the "sour red berry" |
| rose hips | creek, forest edge | red-orange, dried on the stem | food — seeds itch the throat; vitamin C | the manual's page |
| **baneberry** | forest floor, shaded | red (or white) clusters, shrivelled | **poison** — vomiting, weakness for a day | Andrew's "red one that makes you sick" |
| spruce needles / tips | everywhere with spruce | green | tea (warmth, vitamin C) | the safest forage |
| inner bark (spruce, birch cambium) | any live tree, a blade to strip | dormant, dry | famine food — chew or boil; costs the tree | the world remembers a girdled tree |
| chaga | the old birch (rooms.md S7) | the black conk | tea; tinder that holds an ember | a reach puzzle |
| labrador tea | muskeg | leaves persist | tea; mild | crush-and-sniff to identify |
| willow bark | willow bars | dormant | a mild painkiller (tea) | a real thing, weak |

| sweetvetch root ("Eskimo potato") | the bench, river bars | in frozen ground | food — starch; digging frozen ground is the obstacle | near a fire's thawed ground it becomes possible |

## 6. Open questions
1. **Which berries, and how does a player tell them apart?** *(a)* the four edible + baneberry as
   listed, told apart by `examine` prose (colour, bush, smell) and the manual's page; *(b)* fewer.
   **Recommendation: (a)** — five is a real forager's set and one poison keeps it a choice.
2. **The sick berry's effect.** *(a)* vomiting and weakness for a day (calories lost, slower work);
   *(b)* worse. **Recommendation: (a)**, honest and recoverable.
3. **Roots in frozen ground.** *(a)* digging is possible only where ground is thawed (by a fire, at the
   pond edge under water) — the frozen ground is the obstacle; *(b)* always possible, slow.
   **Recommendation: (a)** — it is true, and it makes a fire worth more.
4. **The sling and the rock.** *(a)* rocks are found (the ridge, the creek bar, the muskeg erratic), a
   throw misses with feedback and the odds rise with tries and with a sling; *(b)* simpler fixed odds.
   **Recommendation: (a)** — Andrew's design; the dice are announced ("you feel a dice roll").
5. **Water hemlock.** The one thing in the valley that kills by mistake. *(a)* keep it: eating the root
   is lethal, the manual and its smell warn, and it only matters if someone digs at the marsh — the
   "nothing outright kills" rule is about hazards, not about eating a known poison; *(b)* soften to
   severe illness. **Recommendation: (a)**, but it needs Andrew's word because it is the one death that
   is not the cold.
6. **Ice fishing.** *(a)* in, with the hole as real work and the kit's line; *(b)* out for v1.
   **Recommendation: (a)** — the best meal in the valley should exist.

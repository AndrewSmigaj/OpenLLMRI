# Outside the tail — real-world ontology census

> Official room doc. The breach exit: the torn stump where the tail tore away, opening back into the
> rear cabin one way and out along the crash scar the other. This census maps what is here and the many
> things a person could do with it — as a possibility space, deliberately wide. Gaps are listed flat,
> with no priority implied; the point is the breadth, not a shortlist.

---

## 1. The scene, if it were real
Where the fuselage ends in sheared metal. The tail itself is gone — dragged off northeast, gouging a
scar through the snow. A jerry can and a couple of oil quarts have rolled against the hull; a chunk of
wing-ice sits half-sunk. One way leads back inside through the split hull; the other follows the scar
out into the open.

## 2. Entity census
### Built
| entity | material / state | some of what it affords (not exhaustive) |
|---|---|---|
| jerry can | fuel, sealed | pour, burn, fuel a lamp, soak a rag, an accelerant, a heavy blunt thing |
| oil quarts ×2 | fuel, sealed | burn black (signal smoke), waterproofing, grease, fuel, a rolling weight |
| chunk of ice | ice | melt → water, a cold-pack, a blunt weight, ballast, leave it |

### Scenery (unbuilt nouns)
the sheared fuselage edge (a raw cutting edge; also a hazard), the torn tail stump, the hull side, the
gouged scar (a trail — it goes somewhere), the breach back inside, drifted snow, bent skin panels, the
control cables trailing from the stump.

### Elusive
cold, wind, exposure (it's outside); the scar as a *direction* (out, toward where the tail landed);
sightlines to the treeline, the debris trail, the severed tail; the smell of spilled fuel; the sudden
quiet when the wind drops.

## 3. Things a person could do here → candidate command
A wide net on purpose — much of it is already answerable, which is the interesting part.
| want to… | command | built? |
|---|---|---|
| burn the fuel for a signal fire | `pour oil on X`, `burn X`, `light X` | ~ pour/burn exist; the can is sealed |
| soak something in avgas | `pour fuel on rag`, `pour fuel on bag` | ~ (the tail's sleeping bag is authored already fuel-soaked) |
| melt the ice into water | `melt ice` | ✅ |
| carry fuel/water back inside | `take can`, `go to the rear cabin` | ✅ |
| use the sheared edge to cut | `cut X on edge`, `examine edge` | ❌ (edge is scenery, not a noun) |
| follow the scar to what fell off | `go to the debris trail`, `go to the tail` | ✅ (walk) |
| duck back inside, out of the wind | `go to the rear cabin` | ✅ |
| clock who/what is a few zones off | `look` | ✅ (perception bands) |
| smell the fuel / listen to the wind | `smell`, `listen` | ❌ (no sense verbs) |
| cut a length of trailing cable | `cut cable`, `take cable` | ❌ (scenery) |
| feel how exposed it is out here | (warmth reading exposure) | ❌ |
| drop / stash a load here to come back for | `drop X`, `put X on the hull` | ✅ (space model) |

## 4. What's built / what isn't
Built: three useful objects (two fuel, one water), the space layout (hull_side / the_snow), the walk+see
hub out to the treeline and debris trail and back inside, the perception bands. The fuel is sealed — a
small worked-for friction, likely intentional. Not built, listed flat (no ranking): the sheared edge
and trailing cables as interactables, `smell`/`listen`, examinable scenery nouns, exposure-as-felt-cold,
and the movement effort/time this traversal hub implies. Several of these also showed up in other rooms
— noted as data for later, not as a claim about what the game centers on.
